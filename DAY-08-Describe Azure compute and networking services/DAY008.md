### 1. What is Cloud Computing? (Official Microsoft Definition)

According to Microsoft and standard industry guidelines, **Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services over the internet**—including compute power, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence.

Instead of keeping physical hardware in your own basement or office server room, you rent access to storage and computing power from a cloud provider (like Microsoft Azure) over the internet.

* **Real-World Analogy (Electricity Grid):**
* *On-Premises:* You buy an expensive generator, fuel it, and maintain it on your property.
* *Cloud Computing:* You plug your appliances into the wall socket. A massive utility company miles away handles the generation, maintenance, and distribution, and you pay strictly for the kilowatt-hours you consume.



---

### 2. On-Premises vs. Cloud (The Shift)

Yesterday, you designed an on-prem office with physical servers, switches, and firewalls. Let's look at how the cloud changes each pillar:

| Feature | On-Premises | Cloud Computing (Azure) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hardware Ownership** | You buy, own, and depreciate all physical hardware. | Microsoft owns the hardware; you rent what you need. |
| **Time to Deploy** | Weeks or months (procuring servers, cabling). | Minutes (spinning up a server with a few clicks or code). |
| **Maintenance** | Your IT team replaces failed hard drives and fixes power supplies. | Microsoft handles all physical data center upkeep. |
| **Financial Model** | Heavy upfront **CapEx** (Capital Expenditure). | Flexible, ongoing **OpEx** (Operational Expenditure) via consumption pricing. |

---

### 3. The Consumption-Based Model

One of the most heavily tested concepts on the AZ-900 exam is the **consumption-based model**.

* **What it means:** You do not pay a flat fee just for having access to the cloud. You pay **only for the exact resources you use** during the time you use them (e.g., per-second or per-hour billing for virtual machines, or per-gigabyte billing for storage).
* **Benefits:**
* No upfront infrastructure costs.
* Ability to stop paying for resources when you shut them down (e.g., turning off a test virtual machine over the weekend means you stop paying for compute hours).
* Predictable billing through monitoring tools.



---

### 4. Introduction to Serverless Computing

The study guide specifically highlights **serverless computing**. For a beginner, the name can be confusing—*are there really no servers?*

* **The Reality:** Servers *do* exist, but **you do not manage, provision, patch, or scale them**.
* **How it works:** You write a block of code (a function), upload it to Azure, and tell Azure: *"Run this code only when a user uploads a photo."*
* **The Benefit:** If zero users upload photos today, your serverless cost is **$0**. You pay only for the exact milliseconds your code executes. There is no idle server wasting money.

### 🛠️ 5. Practical Lab: Exploring Azure Pricing & Consumption Models

Let's look at how consumption pricing works in practice using public tools.

#### Lab Steps:

1. Open your web browser and go to the official **[Azure Pricing Calculator](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/calculator/)** (no login required).
2. Click on **Virtual Machines** to add it to your estimate.
3. Look at the billing options:
* Notice the **Pay-as-you-go** option (pure consumption).
* Notice the **1-year or 3-year Reserved Capacity** options (where you commit upfront for a discount).


4. Change the operating system from Windows to Linux and watch how the software licensing cost drops to zero, because Linux is open-source. This is how IT financial planning works in the cloud!

### 🎯 AZ-900 Exam & Foundation Points From Day 8

* **Q1. What is the definition of cloud computing?**
* *Answer:* The delivery of computing services over the internet on a pay-as-you-go pricing model.




* **Q2. What is the consumption-based model?**
* *Answer:* A pricing model where customers pay only for the exact resources they consume, rather than making massive upfront infrastructure investments.


* **Q3. In serverless computing, who manages the underlying server operating systems and hardware maintenance?**
* *Answer:* The cloud provider (Microsoft), allowing developers to focus entirely on writing application code.
